# dyson airwrap: every model compared, what you'll actually pay, and how to cut the price before checkout

People search "dyson airwrap" for one of three reasons. They want to know which of the current models is the right one. They want to know whether a $550–$750 hair tool is defensible. Or they already know which one they want and are hunting for a discount so they don't pay retail.

All three questions have concrete answers, and the third one has a layer most buyers skip entirely.

Dyson doesn't run straight percentage discounts on the Airwrap very often. Retailers do, on a rotating basis. On top of whatever price you land on, there's a cashback layer that works on Dyson's own store, and the size of that layer varies enormously depending on when you buy. That's the part worth planning around.

## Which Airwrap is which

Dyson currently sells three Airwrap families, and the naming is genuinely confusing because the model names changed when the motors did.

**Airwrap Origin** is the entry point at $549.99. It's the same Coanda airflow concept and the same gentle-heat approach, but it ships with a smaller attachment set (a brush, a curling barrel and a dryer head) and it doesn't pair with the MyDyson app. If you want the Airwrap effect without the connected features, this is the one.

**Airwrap i.d.** sits at $649.99 and is the app-connected model. It pairs over Bluetooth with the MyDyson app, where you fill in a hair profile and the tool runs a personalised curl sequence: wrap, style, set, in one button press. Dyson sells it in two versions, Straight+Wavy and Curly+Coily, and the attachment sets differ because the hair types differ.

**Airwrap Co-anda2x** is the flagship at $749.99 for the current editions. It runs Dyson's Gen 2 motor with double the air pressure of earlier models, which is the spec that actually addresses the oldest complaint about the Airwrap: curls that fall out by lunchtime. The 2025 editions of the same tool are still listed, at $699.99.

The Co-anda2x got a meaningful update that landed on July 9, and it's worth knowing about if you're comparing the two price points. The updated version swapped in four attachments: a conical curling barrel for definition closer to the root, a larger round volumizing brush with a 2.8-inch diameter, a 1.6-inch long curling barrel for thicker sections, and the Air straightener 2x, a clamp-style straightening attachment that uses air rather than a hot plate. That straightener is the first of its kind on any Airwrap; previously, if you wanted Dyson's air-straightening tech you had to buy the Airstrait separately at $499.99.

The new attachments are sold individually at $39.99–$44.99 and are backward compatible with the first-generation Co-anda2x, but with no other Airwrap model. So an Airwrap i.d. can't take them.

## How to actually get the cashback tracked

Dyson has unusually strict tracking rules compared with most merchants, and the failure modes are specific. Miss one and the cashback is simply gone, with no way to reconstruct it after the fact.

- **Empty your cart first.** For Dyson specifically, items already sitting in your cart when you arrive from ShopBack won't be tracked. Clear the cart, then click through.
- **Close the Dyson app before you start.** Start the session from ShopBack on the web, not partway inside Dyson's own app.
- **Accept every cookie banner** on Dyson's site. Dyson can only confirm the transaction to ShopBack if you do.
- **Turn off ad blockers and privacy extensions**, or allowlist both sites. Don't open other tabs, don't comparison-shop mid-session, don't click a Google ad or a social media ad between activation and checkout.
- **Finish on the same device you started on.** Don't begin on desktop and complete in the app.
- **One click-through, one order.** If you're buying two units, return to ShopBack and click through again for the second order.
- **If your payment fails, restart from ShopBack.** A failed payment can break the tracking session.
- **Don't apply promo codes that ShopBack doesn't list.** This one deserves its own paragraph, below.

Then leave it alone. Cashback shows as Pending within about 2 days and stays there until Dyson's return window closes, which is the 90-day confirmation window on the US store page. That's normal, not a delay anyone invented: retailers can reverse commissions on returns and cancellations, so the money sits in Pending until it can't be clawed back anymore. Withdrawals need at least $10 in confirmed cashback, run up to $300 a day, and typically process within 10 days.

## What you won't earn cashback on

Read this before you plan the purchase around a rate. On the Dyson store page, the excluded categories are:

- Refurbished items
- Spare parts, replacement parts and accessories
- Gift cards, vouchers and store credit purchases
- Purchases made using vouchers or promo codes that aren't featured on ShopBack
- Fees, taxes, service charges, shipping and delivery

Two of those shape your strategy.

First, **accessories are excluded**. The new Co-anda2x attachments at $39.99–$44.99 each won't earn cashback, so there's no point routing them through a cashback click-through.

Second, **third-party promo codes can void the cashback**. This is the real decision point. If a retailer is offering a $30 new-customer code and ShopBack doesn't list it, you may be choosing between the code and the cashback. Run the numbers: on a $649.99 i.d., a $30 code beats 1.5% cashback ($9.75) easily, and it still beats it at a 5% cashback rate ($32.50 is marginally better, but the tracking risk isn't worth it). Meanwhile, a 13% upsized rate on the same tool is about $84, which no typical new-customer code matches.

The rule that falls out of this: **prioritise the final price first, then treat cashback as the extra layer.** Don't let a high cashback rate talk you out of a genuinely good retailer discount, and don't burn a $30 code chasing $10 of cashback.

## Which one to buy

Straight answers, based on the verified price and feature gaps.

**Airwrap Origin, $549.99.** Right if you want the cheapest legitimate entry into the Airwrap concept and you don't care about app pairing. You get three attachments and no connected styling, so the curl routine is manual. If you already own an Origin, upgrading it isn't worth the money. If it's your first Airwrap, the Strategist's argument is that going up a tier is the better use of a $700-class purchase, since the extra $100 buys you an app-connected tool you'll keep for years.

**Airwrap i.d., $649.99.** The sensible middle. Bluetooth, the MyDyson hair profile, one-touch personalised curl sequence, and six styling functions. It's the model retailers discount most aggressively, and it's the one whose $649.99 list price routinely drops into the $479–$520 range.

**Airwrap Co-anda2x, $749.99 (or $699.99 for the 2025 edition).** The buy if curl longevity is the thing that's been stopping you, because the doubled air pressure is the specific fix for it. The 2025 edition at $699.99 is the smarter pick for anyone who doesn't need the clamp-style straightener, since the motor and air pressure are identical and the attachment lineup is the only material difference.

One more variable: Dyson only builds the Curly+Coily sets in the i.d. and Co-anda2x lines. The Origin ships as one set, so if your hair is curly to coily, the cheaper model isn't a like-for-like comparison. The Curly+Coily sets include a conical barrel, a wave-and-curl diffuser, a wide-tooth comb, a large round volumizing brush and an anti-snag loop brush, which is a different toolkit, not just a different colour.
